# Property-type inputs: Medical, K-12, Multi-Family, Mixed Use

Researched 2026-09-28. Data file: `data/property_types.json`.

## How to read this

- Every broker, insurer and government site was blocked by the sandbox proxy. **Every number below comes from a web-search result excerpt**, not from an opened page. That caps confidence at medium. Before any figure goes into a client-facing report, open the linked source and check it.
- `leak_consequence_multiplier` values are **PAX proposals**. No insurer publishes water or roof-leak claim severity by occupancy relative to a warehouse. The evidence below supports the ranking, not the exact numbers. All are low confidence and capped at 1.5.
- Mixed-use cap rates are not published for any of the five markets. The model should blend them (see below).

Reasoning:

- **retail**: Multiplier scales reactive repair and storm-damage consequence costs relative to a warehouse (1.0). No insurer publishes water/roof-leak claim severity by occupancy relative to warehouses; value is a conservative PAX proposal, capped at 1.5 per model policy. Retail adds inventory/merchandise loss, finished ceilings and fixtures, and business-interruption (lost sales while a store or bay is closed). Tenant typically bears inventory and BI, but landlord bears repair, rent abatement and tenant-relations cost. Kept close to 1.0 because many retail boxes are single-story with simple finishes.
- **office**: Multiplier scales reactive repair and storm-damage consequence costs relative to a warehouse (1.0). No insurer publishes water/roof-leak claim severity by occupancy relative to warehouses; value is a conservative PAX proposal, capped at 1.5 per model policy. Office has finished ceilings (acoustic tile, drywall), carpet, IT/electronics and dense occupancy, so a leak damages more interior value per sq ft and displaces tenants. The one comparative data point found (secondary blog attributing Triple-I 2023: office water claims about 20% above retail) supports office >= retail, so office is set 0.1 above retail.
- **multifamily**: Multiplier scales reactive repair and storm-damage consequence costs relative to a warehouse (1.0). No insurer publishes water/roof-leak claim severity by occupancy relative to warehouses; value is a conservative PAX proposal, capped at 1.5 per model policy. Top-floor units sit directly under the roof: a leak hits occupied living space (drywall, flooring, personal property), may force tenant relocation/rent abatement and unit downtime, and carries mold/habitability liability that is described as the fastest-growing multifamily risk (habitability premiums $375-$1,400/unit, up 300%+ in some markets per Bisnow excerpt). Offsetting factor: many garden-style roofs are steep-slope shingles with lower leak frequency, but frequency is handled elsewhere in the model; this multiplier covers consequence cost only.
- **k12**: Multiplier scales reactive repair and storm-damage consequence costs relative to a warehouse (1.0). No insurer publishes water/roof-leak claim severity by occupancy relative to warehouses; value is a conservative PAX proposal, capped at 1.5 per model policy. Evidence of higher consequence: GAO found about 19% of districts canceled school in 2024-25 due to facility issues (2 million students, about 3.5 million lost learning days), and schools visited had ceiling, floor and electrical damage from roof leaks; EPA requires drying wet materials within 24-48 h or replacing them, and school mold events run from hundreds of thousands to millions (e.g., about $2.3M Nobleboro, ME, 2026). Classrooms have dense finishes, books and electronics; closures carry makeup-day and transport costs; public scrutiny raises remediation standards. Set below medical because schools can often relocate classes and many leaks occur outside occupied hours.
- **medical**: Multiplier scales reactive repair and storm-damage consequence costs relative to a warehouse (1.0). No insurer publishes water/roof-leak claim severity by occupancy relative to warehouses; value is a conservative PAX proposal, capped at 1.5 per model policy. Highest consequence type: any water intrusion over clinical space triggers an Infection Control Risk Assessment (ICRA) and often Class IV containment with negative pressure/HEPA exhaust (CDC, ASHE); sterile supplies, imaging and lab equipment can be lost; ORs, procedure rooms or patient rooms may close (lost revenue); Joint Commission EC.02.06.01 findings follow unsafe conditions. Insurers (Chubb, AXA XL) flag healthcare as a rising, high-severity water-loss class. One case: $200K property damage + $350K lost income from a roof-drain leak at a medical practice (PSQH excerpt). Kept at 1.4 (not 1.5) because the model also covers medical office buildings, where consequences are closer to office; use the high end (1.5) for acute-care hospitals.
- **mixed**: Multiplier scales reactive repair and storm-damage consequence costs relative to a warehouse (1.0). No insurer publishes water/roof-leak claim severity by occupancy relative to warehouses; value is a conservative PAX proposal, capped at 1.5 per model policy. No direct evidence; derived as a blend of the components. The roof sits over the upper use (apartments or office, 1.2-1.25), not the ground-floor retail, so the default follows the upper use: 1.2 (office-over-retail) to 1.25 (apartments-over-retail). Use 1.25 when the upper floors are residential.

### Mixed-use recommendation

No broker publishes a mixed-use cap rate for these markets. Use an income-weighted blend: cap = w_retail x retail cap + (1 - w_retail) x upper-use cap (multifamily for residential-over-retail, office for office-over-retail). Default w_retail = 0.30 (PAX assumption for one retail floor under two to three upper floors; let the rep override). This matches how lenders and appraisers underwrite mixed use: split the rent roll by use, then recombine.

Worked examples, using the existing market retail cap rates and w_retail = 0.30: Hampton Roads 0.3 x 6.55 + 0.7 x 5.4 = about 5.7%; Laurel 0.3 x 6.55 + 0.7 x 5.6 = about 5.9%. Austin, San Antonio and Culbertson need a sourced retail cap rate first (currently null in their market files).

**Medical / Institutional: what the report should emphasize.** Lead with continuity of care and compliance, not property value: avoiding closed ORs, patient rooms and imaging suites; avoiding ICRA containment events and loss of sterile supplies/equipment; keeping Joint Commission Environment of Care (EC.02.06.01) survey findings clean; predictable capital budgets for health-system facilities teams. For investor MOBs, add tenant retention (long leases with health systems) and property value protected.

**K-12 / Education: what the report should emphasize.** Emphasize budget certainty and capital planning: a flat, predictable PaxSeal line item vs unbudgeted emergency repairs; extending roof life to defer bond-funded replacement; protecting instructional days (GAO: about 19% of districts canceled school in 2024-25 for facility problems, 2 million students affected); preventing mold/IAQ events (EPA: dry within 24-48 h); documented condition data for facility-condition assessments and bond planning. Context: national school facility funding gap about $85B/yr (2021 State of Our Schools); 41% of districts need HVAC updates in at least half their schools (GAO-20-494), so roofs compete for scarce capital.

**Multi-Family: what the report should emphasize.** NOI and value: avoided repair spend flows to NOI and is capitalized at a low cap rate. Also stress resident retention (top-floor leaks trigger move-outs and bad reviews), avoided unit downtime/rent abatement, mold and habitability liability (fast-rising insurance line), and lender/insurer roof-age scrutiny (Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac property condition reports).

**Mixed Use: what the report should emphasize.** Same NOI/value story as the dominant upper use, plus: the roof protects the residential/office floors directly, and leaks can cascade to ground-floor retail tenants (business interruption, co-tenancy risk). One maintenance program protects several income streams.

## 4. Typical roof systems

- **Medical / Institutional**: Multi-ply SBS modified bitumen (redundancy over patient areas); Built-up roofing (BUR); Hybrid mod-bit base with TPO/PVC cap; TPO/PVC single-ply on MOBs; Heavy rooftop mechanical equipment, walk pads and frequent traffic.
- **K-12 / Education**: SBS modified bitumen (widely preferred by districts for redundancy); Built-up roofing (BUR) on older buildings; TPO / PVC / EPDM single-ply; Hybrid mod-bit + single-ply; Coating restoration on sound roofs (budget-driven); Standing-seam metal on some newer/steep sections.
- **Multi-Family**: Architectural asphalt shingles on steep-slope garden-style and townhome buildings (most common low-rise type); TPO single-ply on low-slope mid-rise/podium buildings; Modified bitumen / BUR on older low-slope buildings; Low-slope membrane sections over breezeways and stairwells on garden-style buildings; Standing-seam metal accents.
- **Mixed Use**: TPO / PVC single-ply on low-slope podium or wrap buildings; Modified bitumen; Amenity decks / pavers over membrane on podium roofs; Occasional steep-slope shingle or metal accents.
